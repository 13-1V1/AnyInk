Auto-trace

Add a trace image to a character (the button below makes a fake lettering scan), then press Start auto-trace in the Trace image card (or in the Auto-trace card at the bottom of the Trace panel): the sliders redraw a dashed preview on the canvas, and only Replace outlines or Add to outlines changes the glyph (one Undo step).

Worker smoke test

Traces a 64 by 64 black square with a square hole in the potrace worker. Expect one path with two parts (the square and its hole). The first run includes starting the worker.

Track A: image pipeline

Makes a photo-like image (dark letters on paper lit from one side, with grain) with a long side of 1000 or 2000 px and runs autoTraceImageData on it in Scan and Photo mode. Left: the image. Right: what binarize keeps as ink. "Trace this glyph's image" runs autoTraceTrace on the trace image of the glyph above (preview size, then full size).

Track B: shapes, glyph box, apply, export

Puts real potrace output (the unit-test fixtures) into the glyph above, placed like a new trace image: top on the ascent line, as tall as ascent to descent, left edge at 0. Replace and Add are one Undo step each and never change the trace image. "Export and look" builds the OTF with the real exporter and lets Chrome draw the glyph from the font file: every hole must stay paper.

Pipeline bench (no editor)

Pick a PNG, JPEG, or WebP (or make one), then move the sliders. Left: the image. Middle: the black-and-white mask potrace sees. Right: the traced outline (dashed, green) over the image. Everything runs through the real pipeline: binarize.js on this thread, then autoTraceImageData (the potrace worker and svg_to_shapes.js). The Auto-trace card in the Trace panel further down drives the same pipeline through the session.
